# Following the land and its communities

## In short (for people)

District lines can now be asked to follow nature and real communities, in two places:

- **Draw toy:** six new sliders under "Geography & communities": mountains and ridges (USGS elevation), watersheds (USGS river basins), USGS rivers and lakes, drive time (Census road map), towns and school districts (Census). Each has a box that draws the layer on the map (shaded relief, rivers, basin lines, town and school-district outlines, slow-to-drive links).
- **Neutral maps:** four new ensembles beside "counties kept whole": rivers & ridges followed, river basins kept whole, towns kept whole, school districts kept whole (ReCom, links inside weighted 20x, two agreeing chains per state).

Across 44 states (2020 votes, sum of median Democratic seats): plain 228, counties kept whole 230, rivers & ridges 233, river basins 230, towns 228, school districts 229; official maps 221. Following nature or communities moves the neutral result by a few seats nationally; the official maps sit well below every neutral version.
- Not passing the two-chain agreement check: CA (huc20), PA (nature20) (marked in the index).
- Pictures: `docs/samples/<ST>.techniques.geo.png` (the land, the communities, and seven ways to draw).

## For AI agents (detailed)

- Build: `python -m redistricting_addons.geo_layers` (every state with 2+ districts; `--states`, `--workers`) -> `site/data/layers/geo/<ST>.json`, `<ST>.shapes.json`, `<ST>.relief.png`. Sources, formats and thresholds are in the module docstring. Downloads are cached under the redistricting cache (`geo/`): terrain tiles, WBD queries, the USGS 1:1M streams and waterbodies, every county's TIGER roads, Block Assignment Files, code lists, gazetteers. The road step dominates (one Dijkstra per block group on the county road graphs; North Carolina 3.6 million road nodes, about 9 minutes).
- Draw: `site/addons/geo-sliders.js`, loaded by `ensemble-panel.js` on the Draw page. Wraps `DrawEngine.penalize` like `sci-slider.js` (multiplies link lengths by 1 + sum of lambda x factor and adds the same to `edgePen`). Factors: ridge min(1, m / 300); watershed / town / school 1 across a line; river 1 across a river of the chosen Strahler order or a lake; drive time min(3, max(0, r - 1)) with r = link minutes per km / the state's median.
- Ensembles: `python -m redistricting_addons.batch --variants nature20 huc20 town20 school20` -> `site/data/ensembles/<ST>.<variant>.json`. `geo_layers.attach(graph, st, keep)` sets the new `Graph.keep_edges` (nature: links crossing no order-4+ river, lake, 150 m+ ridge or HUC8 line) or `Graph.keep_groups` (huc8 / town / school; -1 never kept), plus `split_groups` and `edge_flags`, so every plan records `huc8_splits`, `town_splits`, `school_splits` and `natural_cut_edges`. The panel offers the four as baselines (site neighbour graph only).
- Report: `python -m redistricting_addons.geo_layers --report`. Pictures: `python -m redistricting_addons.samples --states --fifty "" --techniques-geo NC PA ...`.
- Limits: towns and school districts go to a block group by most of its blocks, not by population (block counts need a Census API key; block shapefiles are 7 GB). The 1:1M river map keeps rivers of map-scale Strahler order 4 or more (smaller creeks are not lines). Ridges are measured along the straight link between population centres (about 240 m elevation pixels), so a long rural link that crosses several ridges records the highest. Edges are the site's Delaunay links, not shared borders.
